Then an important email from your manager sits under fifty others. The fix is not spending longer on email. It is a one-time setup that sorts mail for you, and a short routine each day.</p><p class="prose__intro">This lesson covers both, then shows what Copilot in Outlook adds. You may be using one of three apps: new Outlook for Windows, classic Outlook for Windows, or Outlook on the web. Menu names were checked in October 2026 on Microsoft's support pages, and where the apps differ we say which one. If the steps for new Outlook don't match your screen, you are probably in classic Outlook, so try those steps instead.</p></div><section id="focused-inbox" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="focused-inbox-h"><h2 id="focused-inbox-h" class="lesson-h2">Set up once: Focused Inbox</h2><p>Focused Inbox splits your inbox into two tabs. Focused holds the emails that probably matter to you. Other holds the rest, such as newsletters and automatic messages. You can switch between the tabs at any time, so nothing is lost.</p><ul class="prose-list"><li>New Outlook: View → View settings → Mail → Layout. Choose "Sort messages into Focused and Other", then Save.</li><li>Outlook on the web: Settings → Mail → Layout, then the same option.</li><li>Classic Outlook: on the View tab, select Show Focused Inbox.</li></ul><p>Then teach it. When a newsletter lands in Focused, right-click it and select Move → Move to Other inbox. Choose Always move to Other inbox to send every future email from that sender there. It works the other way too: Always move to Focused inbox for a sender you never want to miss. In classic Outlook the options are called Move to Other and Always Move to Other.</p><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">Shared mailboxes</p><p>Focused Inbox doesn't work on a shared mailbox, such as a team accounts-payable inbox. Use rules there instead (next section).</p></aside></section><section id="rules" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="rules-h"><h2 id="rules-h" class="lesson-h2">Set up once: rules for newsletters and notifications</h2><p>A rule acts on emails as they arrive. Use rules for mail you never need to read in the inbox: newsletters, system notifications and automatic reports. For example, the (invented) "Ledgerline Weekly" newsletter can go straight to a folder called Reading. These steps are for new Outlook:</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Right-click an email from that sender. Point to Rules, then select Create rule.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Choose the folder the emails should go to, and select OK.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Need more than one condition? Select More options instead. Add conditions, actions and exceptions there, then select Save.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>To apply the rule to emails already in your inbox, go to Settings → Mail → Rules and select the run icon next to the rule.</span></li></ol><p>In Outlook on the web, create a rule from Settings → Mail → Rules → Add new rule. In classic Outlook, right-click the email and select Rules → Create Rule, choose a condition and Move the item to folder, and tick "Run this new rule now on messages already in the current folder".</p><aside class="callout callout--warn"><p class="callout__title">Don't hide what you must act on</p><p>Only send mail to a folder when you are sure you don't need to see it on the day. Never move approval requests or emails from your manager out of the inbox. Also note: "Stop processing more rules" is on by default in Outlook on the web, so when two rules match one email, only the first one runs. And new Outlook doesn't run rules for Gmail, Yahoo or iCloud accounts.</p></aside></section><section id="daily-routine" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="daily-routine-h"><h2 id="daily-routine-h" class="lesson-h2">The 15-minute daily routine</h2><p>With the noise sorted, a short routine keeps Focused clear. Do it at the same time each morning. The aim is to decide what each email needs, not to finish all the work.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Open the Focused tab and work from the top. For each email, decide once: reply now if it takes a couple of minutes, flag it, file it or delete it.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Flag anything that needs real work: point at the email in the list and select the flag icon. In new Outlook, flagged emails also appear as tasks in the Flagged email list in To Do (you can turn this list on in To Do settings).</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Add a category to link the email to a project: right-click → Categorize, and choose one. Make a new one from Categorize → New category, for example "Harbor Street project".</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Glance at the Other tab once. Move anything important back to Focused.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">5</span><span>Finish with your flagged emails. Pick the two or three you will complete today.</span></li></ol><div class="table-wrap" role="region" aria-label="Which tool for which kind of email" tabindex="0"><table role="table"><caption>Which tool for which kind of email</caption><thead role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Kind of email</th><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Tool</th><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Why</th></tr></thead><tbody role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Newsletters and system notifications</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">A rule to a folder, or the Other tab</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">They arrive often and rarely need action</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Needs a reply or work from you</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Flag</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">It stays on your follow-up list until it is done</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Belongs to a project or client</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Category</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">You can find all of a project's emails later with one search</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">You handle it the same way every time</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Quick Step</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">One click does several actions</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">A reply that must arrive at a set time</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Schedule send</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">Write it now, deliver it later</td></tr></tbody></table></div><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">Name categories after the work</p><p>Rename the default colour categories to project or client names in Settings → Accounts → Categories (new Outlook and Outlook on the web). "Supplier invoices" tells you more than "Blue category".</p></aside></section><section id="quick-steps-send-search" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="quick-steps-send-search-h"><h2 id="quick-steps-send-search-h" class="lesson-h2">Quick Steps, scheduled send and search</h2><p>A Quick Step does several things in one click, for example moving an email to a folder and marking it as read. Quick Steps exist in new Outlook, classic Outlook and Outlook on the web. Microsoft lists them as only partly available in new Outlook, so some options you know from classic may be missing. In new Outlook and on the web:</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Select Mail. On the Home tab, open the drop-down next to Quick steps and select Manage quick steps.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Select + New quick step and type a name, for example "File to Vendor queries".</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Under Choose an action, pick the first action. Select Add another action for the next one.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>If you like, choose a keyboard shortcut (Ctrl+Shift+5 to 9). Then select Save.</span></li></ol><aside class="callout callout--warn"><p class="callout__title">Quick Steps have no undo</p><p>Ctrl+Z doesn't undo a Quick Step, because some actions, such as permanently deleting, can't be reversed. Try a new Quick Step on one unimportant email first. In classic Outlook, Quick Steps are on the Home tab; choose Create New to make your own.</p></aside><p>Schedule send lets you write now and deliver later, for example a reminder that should arrive on Sunday morning. In new Outlook and Outlook on the web, open the drop-down next to Send, select Schedule send and pick a time. The email waits in Drafts until then. To send it at once, open it in Drafts, select Cancel send, then Send. This doesn't work for IMAP or POP accounts. In classic Outlook, open Message Options in the Tags group (or Options → More commands on the simplified ribbon) and tick "Do not deliver before". The email waits in the Outbox, and Outlook must be online for it to go.</p><p>For search, type these words in the Search box. Microsoft lists them for both classic and new Outlook:</p><ul class="prose-list"><li>from:"Rania Saleh" finds emails from that person.</li><li>subject:"Q3 accruals" finds emails with those words in the subject.</li><li>hasattachment:yes finds emails with attachments.</li><li>received:"last week" finds emails that arrived last week.</li><li>hasflag:true finds your flagged emails, and category:Harbor finds emails with a category whose name includes Harbor.</li><li>Combine them with AND, OR or NOT in capitals: from:"Rania Saleh" AND hasattachment:yes</li></ul><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">Search for whole numbers</p><p>Outlook ignores strings of fewer than five digits, and part of a number won't find the full number. Type the whole invoice or PO number. If you get too many results, add another word: Outlook only shows the first 1,000.</p></aside></section><section id="copilot" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="copilot-h"><h2 id="copilot-h" class="lesson-h2">What Copilot in Outlook can do</h2><p>Copilot is the AI assistant built into Outlook. It can save you reading long threads and typing first drafts, but it doesn't decide for you. For how to write the email itself, see Lesson 2. According to Microsoft's support pages, it can:</p><ul class="prose-list"><li>Summarise a thread: select Summary by Copilot at the top of the thread (new Outlook) or Summarize (classic Outlook). The summary can include numbered links to the emails it came from.</li><li>Draft an email: in a new email, select the Copilot icon, then Draft (new Outlook) or Draft with Copilot (classic). Type what you want, select Generate, then Keep it, or Retry for another version.</li><li>Coach you: after you write an email, select the Copilot icon and Coaching by Copilot. It suggests changes to tone, clarity and reader sentiment. Use Apply all suggestions, or take only the ones you agree with.</li><li>Set up a meeting: in new Outlook, Schedule with Copilot turns an email thread into a meeting invitation, with the people on the thread as attendees.</li></ul><p>Which plans include it? Microsoft says everyone with a qualifying Microsoft 365 business or enterprise plan has Copilot in Outlook, signed in with a work or school account. Its comparison lists Copilot in Outlook as included with or without the Microsoft 365 Copilot add-on licence. The add-on, assigned by your admin, lets Copilot also use your chats, documents and other work data. The home plans Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium include Copilot in the Outlook desktop app too, within a limit of AI credits. A summary can miss a late correction to an amount or a date. Open the cited email before you post an entry or reply with a number, and follow your company's rules on confidential data (Lesson 5).</p></aside><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">Draft with Copilot not working?</p><p>In new Outlook and Outlook on the web, Draft with Copilot doesn't work on plain-text emails. Switch to HTML in Settings → Mail → Compose and reply.</p></aside></section><section id="mistakes" class="lesson-section" aria-labelledby="mistakes-h"><h2 id="mistakes-h" class="lesson-h2">Common mistakes</h2><ul class="mistakes" role="list"><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Rules that hide important mail</p><p>Moving everything out of the inbox means something gets missed. Only automate senders you know well, and look through those folders once a week.</p></li><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Using the inbox as a to-do list</p><p>An email you read and leave disappears among the others. Flag it, file it or answer it.</p></li><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Trusting a summary without opening the thread</p><p>Copilot can miss a late change. Check amounts, dates and names in the original emails.</p></li></ul></section><section id="practice" class="lesson-section practice" aria-labelledby="practice-h"><h2 id="practice-h" class="lesson-h2">Try it now: 5 minutes</h2><p>Try it with a real task from your week. Keep this page open in another tab.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Turn on Focused Inbox and move two senders to Other with Always move to Other inbox.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Create one rule for a newsletter or notification you get every week, and run it on the emails already there.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Create three categories for your main projects, and one Quick Step that files an email and marks it as read.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Tomorrow morning, do the 15-minute routine. If you have Copilot, try Summary by Copilot on your longest thread and check it against the emails.</span></li></ol></section><figure class="takeaway"><p class="small-title">Remember this</p><blockquote><p>Set Outlook up once: Focused Inbox, a few rules and Quick Steps.
