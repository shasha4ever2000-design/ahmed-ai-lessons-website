<section class="learn" aria-labelledby="learn-title"><h2 id="learn-title" class="small-title">In this lesson you'll learn</h2><ul role="list"><li><svg width="18" height="18" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false" class="icon"><path d="M5 12.5l4.5 4.5L19 7.5"></path></svg><span>How Heading styles turn a long document into something you can rearrange</span></li><li><svg width="18" height="18" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false" class="icon"><path d="M5 12.5l4.5 4.5L19 7.5"></path></svg><span>How to add a contents page, captions and page numbers that update with one click</span></li><li><svg width="18" height="18" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false" class="icon"><path d="M5 12.5l4.5 4.5L19 7.5"></path></svg><span>What Copilot in Word can do, and how to get an outline from AI safely</span></li></ul></section><div class="prose"><p class="prose__intro">A 20-page report is easy to write and hard to change. Move one section or add one table, and the page numbers, the contents page and every "see Table 4" are suddenly wrong. Word can keep all of that in order for you, as long as you build the report the way Word expects.</p><p class="prose__intro">This lesson covers the mechanics: Heading styles, the Navigation Pane, an automatic contents page, captions, page numbers, and Arabic with English in one file. Then it shows what Copilot in Word can do. For writing the content itself, see Lesson 10. Menu names were checked in October 2026 in Word for Microsoft 365 on Windows. Word changes often, so if a button has moved, look for the same words.</p></div><section id="heading-styles" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="heading-styles-h"><h2 id="heading-styles-h" class="lesson-h2">Heading styles, not bold</h2><p>The most common habit in long reports causes the most trouble later: making a title bold and bigger by hand. Word sees that as normal text. It can't put it in a contents page, show it in the Navigation Pane or restyle it for you. Heading styles fix all three.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Select the text of a main section title, for example "3. Warehouse".</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>On the Home tab, in the Styles gallery, choose Heading 1.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>For sub-sections, such as "3.1 Stock counts", choose Heading 2. Select More at the edge of the gallery to see Heading 3 and other styles.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Do this for every title in the report. Body text stays Normal.</span></li></ol><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">Change the look once</p><p>To restyle every Heading 1 at once, format one heading the way you want (font, size, colour). Then right-click Heading 1 in the Styles gallery and choose Update Heading 1 to Match Selection. Every Heading 1 in the report changes. Modify, on the same menu, opens all the settings.</p></aside></section><section id="navigation-pane" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="navigation-pane-h"><h2 id="navigation-pane-h" class="lesson-h2">Move sections with the Navigation Pane</h2><p>Once your headings are styled, the Navigation Pane shows the report as an outline. You can jump to any section, or move a whole section without cut and paste.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>On the View tab, tick Navigation Pane, or press Ctrl+F.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Select Headings at the top of the pane. Every Heading 1, 2 and 3 appears in order.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Click a heading to jump to it. Use the small arrow beside a heading to fold or unfold its sub-headings.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Drag a heading up or down to move the section. Everything under it (text, tables, charts) moves with it.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">5</span><span>Right-click a heading to change its level or add a new heading.</span></li></ol><p>Example: in a made-up "2026 Operations Review" for Wadi Stationery Co., the manager wants Risks to come before Next year's plan. Drag Risks up one place, then update the contents page (next section). One thing to know: headings inside tables, text boxes, headers or footers don't show in the pane.</p></section><section id="contents-captions-numbers" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="contents-captions-numbers-h"><h2 id="contents-captions-numbers-h" class="lesson-h2">Contents page, captions and page numbers</h2><p>With headings in place, three automatic parts do the rest. Each one updates by itself or with one click, so you never retype a page number.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Click where the contents page should go, usually right after the cover page.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Go to References → Table of Contents and choose Automatic Table 1 or Automatic Table 2.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>For each table or chart: select it, go to References → Insert Caption, pick a Label (Table or Figure), type the text after the number, for example "Sales by region", and select OK.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>For page numbers: go to Insert → Page Number and choose where they go, for example the bottom of the page. To hide the number on the cover, tick Different First Page on the Header &amp; Footer tab. When you are done, select Close Header and Footer or press Esc.</span></li></ol><div class="table-wrap" role="region" aria-label="What to refresh before you send" tabindex="0"><table role="table"><caption>What to refresh before you send</caption><thead role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Part</th><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Add it</th><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Refresh it</th></tr></thead><tbody role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Contents page</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">References → Table of Contents</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">References → Update Table → Update entire table</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Table and figure captions</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">References → Insert Caption</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">Press Ctrl+A, then F9 (or right-click → Update Field)</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">List of tables or figures</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">References → Insert Table of Figures</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">Click it, then References → Update Table</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Page numbers</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Insert → Page Number</td><td role="cell" dir="auto">Nothing to do: they follow the pages</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p>Update page numbers only changes the numbers and ignores any change to heading text. Update entire table also picks up headings you renamed or moved. If in doubt, choose Update entire table.</p><aside class="callout callout--warn"><p class="callout__title">Skip the manual contents table</p><p>The Table of Contents gallery also has a manual style. It doesn't use your headings, so it never updates itself and you would type every line. Always pick an automatic one, and make updating the contents page your last step before you save as PDF or send.</p></aside></section><section id="arabic-and-english" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="arabic-and-english-h"><h2 id="arabic-and-english-h" class="lesson-h2">Arabic and English in one document</h2><p>Many reports in the region need both languages: an Arabic summary with English tables, or the other way round. Word sets the direction for each paragraph, so one document can hold both.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Add Arabic as an editing language, once: File → Options → Language. Under Office authoring languages, select Add a Language and choose Arabic.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Turn on an Arabic keyboard in Windows so you can type it.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Click in a paragraph. On the Home tab, in the Paragraph group, select Right-to-left for Arabic or Left-to-right for English. These buttons appear once both kinds of language are enabled.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Repeat for each paragraph that changes language, headings included.</span></li></ol><p>You don't need Set as Preferred. If you choose it, Arabic becomes the default editing language in all your Microsoft 365 apps.</p><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">One paragraph, one direction</p><p>A short English word inside an Arabic sentence, like a product name or "VAT", is fine. A whole English sentence reads better in its own left-to-right paragraph. Long mixed sentences in one paragraph are what usually make full stops and brackets land in odd places.</p></aside></section><section id="copilot-and-ai" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="copilot-and-ai-h"><h2 id="copilot-and-ai-h" class="lesson-h2">Copilot in Word, and an outline from any AI</h2><p>Copilot is Microsoft's AI assistant inside Word. To open it, select the Copilot button in the corner of your document (Microsoft calls it the Copilot Dynamic Action Button). A chat pane opens on the right. According to Microsoft's help pages, it can:</p><ul class="prose-list"><li>Draft: start a draft from a prompt, an outline, your notes or a file you point it to.</li><li>Rewrite: select text, choose Copilot from the small toolbar that appears, then pick Auto Rewrite or describe the change. Then choose Replace, Insert below or Regenerate.</li><li>Summarise: give a summary of the document, or answer your questions about it.</li></ul><p>Which plans include it? Microsoft says Copilot in Word needs an eligible Microsoft 365 subscription or a Microsoft Copilot licence. For personal use, Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium include AI credits for it (on Family and Premium, only the subscription owner can use them). At work, it depends on the licence your company bought and its settings. If you don't see Copilot in Word, your plan may not include it or your organisation may have turned it off.</p><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">Try Arabic first</p><p>Microsoft notes that Copilot supports fewer languages than Word itself. Before you rely on it for an Arabic section, test it on one short paragraph.</p></aside><p>No Copilot? ChatGPT or Claude can still help with the structure. You now have the skeleton of the report. For writing the text itself (your facts, your voice, no invented numbers), see Lesson 10.</p><aside class="callout callout--warn"><p class="callout__title">Give AI the shape, not the report</p><p>Don't paste the draft report, real figures, customer names or salaries into a personal ChatGPT or Claude account. If your company gives you Copilot with a work account, follow its rules on what you can use it for. Lesson 5 has a simple traffic-light rule.</p></aside></section><section id="mistakes" class="lesson-section" aria-labelledby="mistakes-h"><h2 id="mistakes-h" class="lesson-h2">Common mistakes</h2><ul class="mistakes" role="list"><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Bold instead of Heading styles</p><p>A bold title looks like a heading, but Word can't see it. It won't appear in the contents page or the Navigation Pane.</p></li><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Sending with an old contents page</p><p>Moved sections and new pages make the contents page wrong until you update it. Make References → Update Table → Update entire table your last step.</p></li><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Typing "Table 3" by hand</p><p>Add a table in the middle and every number after it is wrong. Use Insert Caption and Word keeps the numbers in order.</p></li></ul></section><section id="practice" class="lesson-section practice" aria-labelledby="practice-h"><h2 id="practice-h" class="lesson-h2">Try it now: 5 minutes</h2><p>Try it with a real task from your week. Keep this page open in another tab.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Open a long document you use, or make a practice one with five made-up section titles.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>Apply Heading 1 and Heading 2 to every title, then open the Navigation Pane and move one section.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Insert an automatic contents page, add one caption and page numbers, then update the contents page.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Ask ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot for an outline with the prompt above, using no real names or numbers, and compare it with your own structure.</span></li></ol></section><figure class="takeaway"><p class="small-title">Remember this</p><blockquote><p>Style your headings and Word does the rest: the outline, the contents page, captions and page numbers. Update before you send, and give AI the shape of the report, not its secrets.</p></blockquote></figure>
