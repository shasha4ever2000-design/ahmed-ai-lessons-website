An automation turns that around: something happens (a form is submitted, an email arrives) and the AI does its part without you opening a chat.</p><p class="prose__intro">You don't need to code. Tools like Zapier, Make and Microsoft Power Automate connect apps with clicks. This lesson builds one small, useful automation and shows how to keep it under control.</p></div><section id="pick-a-task" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="pick-a-task-h"><h2 id="pick-a-task-h" class="lesson-h2">Pick a boring, repeated task</h2><p>A good first automation is small, frequent and low-risk. If it goes wrong, nothing bad happens and you can fix it quickly.</p><div class="table-wrap" role="region" aria-label="Good and bad first automations" tabindex="0"><table role="table"><caption>Good and bad first automations</caption><thead role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Task</th><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Good first automation?</th></tr></thead><tbody role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Summarise each new form reply into a spreadsheet</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Yes: small, frequent and easy to check</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Sort incoming requests into categories</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Yes: easy to review</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Draft (not send) replies to common questions</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">With care: a person reads every draft before it goes out</td></tr><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Send emails or payments with no one checking</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">No: too risky for a first try</td></tr></tbody></table></div></section><section id="the-pattern" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="the-pattern-h"><h2 id="the-pattern-h" class="lesson-h2">The pattern: trigger → AI step → action</h2><p>Almost every AI automation has three parts:</p><ul class="prose-list"><li>Trigger: the event that starts it. "A new form reply arrives."</li><li>AI step: what the AI does with it. "Summarise the reply and mark it urgent or not."</li><li>Action: where the result goes. "Add a row to a spreadsheet."</li></ul><aside class="callout callout--tip"><p class="callout__title">Write it as one sentence first</p><p>"When [trigger], use AI to [AI step], then [action]." If you can't write that sentence, the automation isn't clear enough to build yet.</p></aside></section><section id="build-one" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="build-one-h"><h2 id="build-one-h" class="lesson-h2">Build one: form replies into a summary sheet</h2><p>Here is the example in Zapier. Make and Power Automate have the same three parts, with different button names.</p><ol class="steps"><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>Create a Google Sheet with four columns: Date, Name, Summary and Urgent.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">2</span><span>In Zapier, create a new Zap. For the trigger, choose Google Forms → "New Form Response", connect your account, pick your form, and test the trigger so Zapier loads a sample reply.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">3</span><span>Add a step and choose "AI by Zapier". Write the prompt below, insert the form fields where the brackets are, and add two output fields: Summary and Urgent.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">4</span><span>Add an action: Google Sheets → "Create Spreadsheet Row". Fill Date and Name from the form, and Summary and Urgent from the AI step.</span></li><li><span class="steps__n" aria-hidden="true">5</span><span>Test each step with the sample reply and check the new row in your sheet. Then publish the Zap.</span></li></ol><figure class="sheet prompt-card prompt-card--plain"><div class="sheet__head"><figcaption class="sheet__label">Prompt for the AI step</figcaption><button type="button" class="btn btn--quiet btn--sm copy-btn" aria-label="Copy: Prompt for the AI step"><svg width="18" height="18" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false" class="icon"><rect x="9" y="9" width="11" height="11" rx="2"></rect><path d="M5 15V6a2 2 0 0 1 2-2h9"></path></svg>Copy</button><span class="copy-status visually-hidden" role="status" aria-live="polite"></span></div><p class="sheet__text" dir="auto">Summarise this form reply in one sentence for a busy team leader. Then answer Urgent with only "yes" or "no". It is urgent only if the person reports something broken or asks for help within 2 days.

Reply:
[form answer fields]</p><p class="prompt-card__note">Clear rules for "urgent" give consistent labels. Vague rules give random ones.</p></figure></section><section id="human-check" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="human-check-h"><h2 id="human-check-h" class="lesson-h2">Keep a human check</h2><p>An automation runs when you're not looking. Build in a point where a person looks at the result before anything important happens.</p><ul class="prose-list"><li>Let the AI write to a sheet or a draft, not straight to a customer.</li><li>For the first couple of weeks, compare every row with the original reply.</li><li>Add a "Checked by" column so the team knows what has been reviewed.</li></ul><aside class="callout callout--warn"><p class="callout__title">Know what you send to the AI</p><p>Every run sends the form data to an AI service. Don't automate forms that collect health, money or other sensitive personal details without your company's approval.</p></aside></section><section id="costs-and-limits" class="prose lesson-section" aria-labelledby="costs-and-limits-h"><h2 id="costs-and-limits-h" class="lesson-h2">Costs and limits</h2><p>Automation tools count what you use. Check before you build, so there are no surprises.</p><div class="table-wrap" role="region" aria-label="What to check (Zapier, September 2026)" tabindex="0"><table role="table"><caption>What to check (Zapier, September 2026)</caption><thead role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Question</th><th scope="col" role="columnheader">Answer</th></tr></thead><tbody role="rowgroup"><tr role="row"><th scope="row" role="rowheader">Can I build this on the free plan?</th><td role="cell" dir="auto">Not fully. The free plan allows Zaps with one trigger and one action, and 100 tasks a month. You'll find the odd cases before the automation does.</p></li><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Vague instructions in the AI step</p><p>The AI step runs the same prompt again and again. Clear rules and a fixed output keep the results consistent.</p></li><li class="mistakes__item"><p class="mistakes__title">Set and forget</p><p>Forms, tools and prices change. Look at the results every week, and switch the automation off if something looks wrong.</p></li></ul></section><section id="practice" class="lesson-section practice" aria-labelledby="practice-h"><h2 id="practice-h" class="lesson-h2">Try it now: 5 minutes</h2><p>Try it with a real task from your week.
