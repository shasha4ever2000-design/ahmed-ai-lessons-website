Month-end close#01

Build a reusable close checklist

When your close lives in your head or in old emails.

You are a finance manager. Create a month-end close checklist for a small company with [number] entities. Group tasks by day (Day -2 to Day +5). For each task give: task, owner role, due day, and what "done" looks like. Use a table. Ask me 3 questions first if anything is unclear.

Your check: Add your own company-specific steps; AI won't know them.

Month-end close#02

List accruals you might be missing

Before you lock the period.

Here is a list of our usual monthly expenses (made-up example): rent, electricity, internet, cleaning, consultant fees, staff bonus, software subscriptions. Which of these often need an accrual at month-end and why? For each one, tell me what document I should look for to support the amount. Do not invent amounts.

Your check: Confirm every accrual against a real invoice, contract or email.

Month-end close#03

Explain a journal entry in plain words

When a manager or auditor asks "what is this entry?"

Explain this journal entry in 3 short sentences for a non-finance manager. Say what happened, why we booked it, and what it does to profit. Debit: [account] [amount] Credit: [account] [amount] Description: [short description]

Your check: Make sure the explanation matches the real reason for the entry.

Month-end close#04

Write the month-end close email

When the close is done and you need to tell everyone.

Write a short close-completed email to the finance team and managers. Facts: period [month], closed on [date], [number] open items still being worked on: [list]. Tone: clear and calm. Use bullet points. Under 120 words.

Your check: Double-check the dates and the open items list before sending.

Month-end close#05

Run a close retrospective

After a close that felt too slow.

Our close took [number] days. These were the delays: [list delays]. Group them into causes (people, data, systems, approvals). For each cause, suggest one small change we can try next month. Keep it practical, no big system projects.

Your check: Pick one or two ideas only; don't change everything at once.

Reconciliations#06

Find why two balances don't match

When the bank, ledger or sub-ledger is off by an amount.

My ledger balance is [amount] and the bank statement shows [amount]. The difference is [amount]. List the most common reasons for a difference like this, in order of how likely they are. For each reason, tell me exactly what to look for in the statement or ledger.

Your check: AI gives you places to look; only the documents give the answer.

Reconciliations#07

Spot a transposition error

When the difference looks like a typing mistake.

The difference in my reconciliation is [amount]. Could this be a transposition (two digits swapped) or a slide (decimal in the wrong place)? Explain the quick test in simple steps, then show me which original amounts could produce this difference.

Your check: Recalculate any suggestion yourself; AI can slip on arithmetic.

Reconciliations#08

Write a clear reconciliation note

When reviewers ask for the story behind the recon.

Write a reconciliation note for [account name] at [date]. Ledger: [amount]. Supporting balance: [amount]. Reconciling items: [list with amounts]. Unexplained: [amount]. Format: one-line summary, a small table of items, and next steps with owners. Use only these numbers.

Your check: Make sure the items add up to the difference before you file it.

Reconciliations#09

Plan an intercompany match-up

When two related companies show different balances with each other.

Company A shows [amount] due from Company B. Company B shows [amount] due to Company A. Give me a step-by-step plan to match the two sides: what reports to pull, how to line them up, the usual causes (timing, FX, missing invoices), and a simple table layout to track open items.

Your check: Agree the final position with the other side in writing.

Reconciliations#10

Turn an aging report into actions

When receivables or payables are piling up.

Here is a summary of our receivables aging (made-up): 0-30 days [amount], 31-60 [amount], 61-90 [amount], 90+ [amount], top 3 late customers: [Customer A], [Customer B], [Customer C]. Suggest a follow-up plan by bucket, who should act, and a short polite reminder for the 90+ customers.

Your check: Check each customer's real history before sending any reminder.

Variance & commentary#11

Explain the three biggest changes

When management wants the month in a few lines.

Here are this month's and last month's totals (made-up): Line | Last month | This month Revenue | 500,000 | 460,000 Salaries | 180,000 | 182,000 Marketing | 40,000 | 65,000 Write three short paragraphs on the biggest changes for managers. Use only these numbers. Give possible reasons as questions for me to confirm, not as facts.

Your check: Every number must match your sheet; keep only reasons you can confirm.

Variance & commentary#12

Budget vs actual in plain words

When a department head asks "are we over budget?"

Department: [name]. Budget for the year so far: [amount]. Actual so far: [amount]. Biggest lines over budget: [list]. Write a 5-line summary for the department head: are they over or under, by how much and what %, the main drivers, and one question they should answer. Show your % calculation.

Your check: Recheck the % yourself; it's the line people quote back to you.

Variance & commentary#13

Turn numbers into a one-page summary

Before a monthly management meeting.

Using only the figures below, write a one-page management summary with these headings: Headline, What went well, What needs attention, Cash, Next month. Short sentences. No jargon. [paste a small table of key figures]

Your check: Remove company names and real figures if your policy requires it.

Variance & commentary#14

Find the story in a trend

When you have 12 months of data and no time.

Here are 12 months of [metric] (made-up): [Jan value, Feb value, ...]. Describe the trend in 3 bullet points: overall direction, any unusual month, and any pattern (like a seasonal peak). Then suggest the best chart type to show it and why.

Your check: Look at the chart yourself; AI can miss what your eye catches.

Variance & commentary#15

Prepare for tough questions

Before you present results to your manager or the board.

I'm presenting these results: [short summary]. Act as a sharp CFO. Ask me the 7 hardest questions you would ask, from most to least likely. After I answer each one, tell me if my answer is clear enough.

Your check: Practise out loud; reading answers is not the same as saying them.

Excel & data#16

Get the right formula

When you know what you want but not the formula.

In Excel, column A has invoice numbers, column B has amounts, column C has customer names. On another sheet I have a list of customer names in column A. Give me a formula for column B that totals each customer's invoices. Explain it in one line, and give a version for older Excel without new functions.

Your check: Test the formula on 3 rows you can check by hand.

Excel & data#17

Fix a formula that shows an error

When you see #N/A, #VALUE! or #REF!.

This Excel formula returns [error]: [paste formula]. The data looks like this: [describe columns and 2 sample rows]. Tell me the 3 most likely causes, how to check each one, and the corrected formula.

Your check: Hidden spaces and numbers stored as text cause most errors; check those first.

Excel & data#18

Clean a messy export

When a system export has merged cells, blank rows and odd dates.

I exported a report from our accounting system into Excel. Problems: blank rows between groups, headers repeated on every page, dates as text like "03.04.2026", and amounts with "CR" at the end for credits. Give me step-by-step instructions (Power Query or formulas) to turn it into a clean flat table.

Your check: Work on a copy and compare totals before and after cleaning.

Excel & data#19

Design a pivot table

When you have a long list and need a summary.

My data has these columns: Date, Branch, Expense type, Supplier, Amount. I need to see monthly spend by branch and expense type, and the top 5 suppliers. Tell me exactly what to put in Rows, Columns, Values and Filters, and how to group dates by month.

Your check: Check the pivot grand total equals the total of your source data.

Excel & data#20

Build a simple check sheet

When you want your workbook to catch its own mistakes.

I have a workbook with a trial balance sheet and a summary report sheet. Suggest 5 simple check formulas I can put on a "Checks" tab (for example: debits equal credits, report total equals TB total). For each, give the formula and how to make it show "OK" or "CHECK".

Your check: Break one number on purpose to confirm each check actually works.

Emails & communication#21

Chase a late document politely

When you need something from another team before the deadline.

Write a short, polite but firm email to [team/role] asking for [document] by [date] so we can finish the close. This is the second reminder. Offer to help if something is blocking them. Under 90 words. Then give me the same email in Arabic that sounds natural, not translated.

Your check: Read it as the receiver; would you feel respected or pushed?

Emails & communication#22

Say no to a request (nicely)

When someone asks for a payment or change you can't approve.

Write a polite email declining a request to [describe request]. Reason: [reason, e.g. missing approval / outside policy]. Explain what they need to do to get it approved. Warm tone, no blame, under 100 words.

Your check: Make sure the policy you mention is the real one.

Emails & communication#23

Explain a number to a non-finance colleague

When sales or HR asks why their number looks different.

A sales manager asks why the revenue in the finance report ([amount]) is lower than their sales report ([amount]). Possible reasons: returns, discounts, and orders not yet invoiced. Write a friendly reply that explains this in simple words, with a tiny example, and offers a quick call.

Your check: Only include the reasons that actually apply to your numbers.

Emails & communication#24

Turn meeting notes into actions

Right after a finance or project meeting.

Here are my notes from today's meeting. Pull out: 1) decisions, 2) actions as a table with Action, Owner and Due date, 3) open questions. Use only what is in the notes. If an owner or date is missing, write "not agreed". Then draft a short follow-up email. Notes: [paste notes]

Your check: Check owners and dates; make sure nothing was added.

Emails & communication#25

Rewrite a message to sound professional

When your draft feels too blunt or too long.

Rewrite this message so it sounds professional and friendly. Keep my meaning and all facts exactly the same. Make it shorter if you can. Show me the new version, then list what you changed in 3 bullets. [paste your draft]

Your check: Read it once more; make sure it still sounds like you.

Audit, controls & study#26

Prepare for an audit request

When auditors send a long list of requests.

Our auditors sent this request list: [paste list]. Turn it into a tracker table with: request, document needed, likely source/team, owner, due date, status. Group similar requests together and flag any that look unclear so I can ask the auditors.

Your check: Agree the deadlines with the auditors; don't rely on AI's guesses.

Audit, controls & study#27

Find control gaps in a process

When you want to tighten a process like payments or purchasing.

Here is how our [process, e.g. supplier payment] works today: [describe steps and who does each]. Act as an internal auditor. List the risks you see (fraud, error, delay), the control that would reduce each one, and rate each risk High / Medium / Low with one line of reasoning.

Your check: Discuss changes with the process owner before adding new steps.

Audit, controls & study#28

Write a simple policy or procedure

When a process has no written steps.

Write a one-page procedure for [process] at a small company. Sections: Purpose, Who it applies to, Steps (numbered, with the role responsible), Approvals, Records to keep. Plain language. Ask me 3 questions first about how we work today.

Your check: Have the people who do the work read it before it's approved.

Audit, controls & study#29

Make a CMA study plan

When you're starting a CMA part and feel lost.

I'm studying for CMA Part [1 or 2]. My exam is on [date]. I can study [hours] hours on weekdays and [hours] on weekends. Make a week-by-week plan that covers each section, leaves the last 3 weeks for practice questions and mock exams, and includes one rest day a week. Use a table.

Your check: Check section names and weights against the official exam outline.

Audit, controls & study#30

Get quizzed on your notes

When you want to check what you really remember.

Here are my notes on [topic]: [paste notes]. Quiz me with 10 questions, one at a time. Mix multiple choice and short calculations. Wait for my answer before the next question. At the end, tell me my score and the 2 areas I should review.

Your check: Use questions from your notes; verify any answer AI marks as "correct".
